RoboFleet Guard · Runtime safety for learned robot policies

Every action a learned policy takes, checked before it reaches the robot.

VLA and reinforcement-learning policies are probabilistic. They spike, drift past joint limits, and act confidently on observations they were never trained on. RoboFleet Guard sits between the policy and the robot and returns a verdict on every control step: pass it, correct it, or stop it, with a tamper-evident record of why.

Where it sits

A safety layer in the control loop, not another dashboard.

The policy publishes what it wants to do. The guard decides what actually reaches the robot. It never generates motion of its own: it only passes, narrows, or stops what the policy proposed.

Three verdicts

Correct what can be corrected. Stop what can't.

PASS

The action is inside the safety envelope.

Robot receives: the action, unchanged.
CLAMP

A joint target is out of range, or the step is faster than the policy's own demonstrations ever moved.

Robot receives: a corrected action inside the envelope.
HALT

NaN or infinite output, effort or workspace breach, an out-of-distribution observation, or a policy that keeps pushing past its limits.

Robot receives: its last safe command. The guard stays latched until a named operator resets it.

Envelope from your data

Limits derived from what the policy was trained on.

Point the guard at the LeRobot v3 dataset behind a policy, including datasets RoboFleet Nexus exports directly from fleet telemetry. It derives joint ranges, per-step rate limits, and an observation baseline, giving a team a reviewed starting envelope instead of a blank YAML file.

robofleet guard fit-baseline ~/robofleet_datasets/arm_01/ --out guard_config/

Evidence, not logs

A record a safety reviewer can verify.

Every clamp, halt and operator reset is written to a SHA-256 hash chain: what the policy proposed, what the guard sent instead, and which rule fired. Editing any past record breaks the chain, and one command proves whether it's intact.

robofleet guard verify-audit audit.jsonl
Chain intact: 15 records verified.

Safety reviewers

People who must sign off on a learned controller and need evidence of what it did and what was stopped.

Policy researchers

Researchers who want failure cases captured automatically as data, not reconstructed from memory.

The current boundary

What it is today, and what it isn't.

  • A software layer. It does not replace hardware e-stops, a certified safety controller, or functional-safety engineering.
  • Validated with a scripted policy and simulated arm over ROS2. Integration with a pretrained LeRobot policy in Isaac Sim is in progress.
  • Out-of-distribution detection is per-joint statistics. It catches gross faults like a slipped encoder; subtler distribution shift needs richer models.
  • Derived envelopes are a starting point. They must be reviewed before use; they are not certified limits.
