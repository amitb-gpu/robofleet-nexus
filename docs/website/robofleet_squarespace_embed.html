A Safety Layer in the Control Loop

The policy publishes what it wants to do. The guard decides what actually reaches the robot. It never generates motion of its own: it only passes, narrows, or stops what the policy proposed.

Three Verdicts on Every Action

Correct what can be corrected. Stop what can't. Record both.

PASS

The action is inside the safety envelope.

Robot receives the action, unchanged.

CLAMP

A joint target is out of range, or the step is faster than the policy's own demonstrations ever moved.

Robot receives a corrected action inside the envelope.

HALT

NaN or infinite output, an effort or workspace breach, an out-of-distribution observation, or a policy that keeps pushing past its limits.

Robot holds its last safe command until a named operator resets the guard.

Limits From Your Data, Evidence for Your Reviewers

Envelope derived from training data

Point the guard at the LeRobot v3 dataset behind a policy, including datasets RoboFleet Nexus exports directly from fleet telemetry. It derives joint ranges, per-step rate limits and an observation baseline: a reviewed starting envelope instead of a blank config file.

robofleet guard fit-baseline ./arm_01_dataset --out guard_config/

A record a safety reviewer can verify

Every clamp, halt and operator reset is written to a SHA-256 hash chain: what the policy proposed, what the guard sent instead, and which rule fired. Editing any past record breaks the chain, and one command proves whether it is intact.

robofleet guard verify-audit audit.jsonl Chain intact: 15 records verified.

Important boundary RoboFleet Guard is a software layer. It does not replace hardware e-stops, a certified safety controller, or functional-safety engineering. Its out-of-distribution check uses per-joint statistics: it catches gross faults such as a slipped encoder, while subtler distribution shift needs richer models. Derived envelopes are a starting point that must be reviewed, not certified limits.
